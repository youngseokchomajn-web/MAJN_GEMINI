# MAJUNG E-001-E — Bloch-Salisbury 2023 RCT Primary Extraction

- Citation: Bloch-Salisbury E, Wilson JD, Rodriguez N, et al. Efficacy of a Vibrating Crib Mattress to Reduce Pharmacologic Treatment in Opioid-Exposed Newborns: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Pediatrics. 2023;177(7):665-674.
- DOI: 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2023.1077
- PMID: 37184872
- PMCID: PMC10186209
- Evidence Type: Randomized clinical trial
- Population: 208 term newborns with prenatal opioid exposure; 181 completed hospitalization at study sites and were analyzed
- Directness to MAJN engineering: Moderate
- Directness to ordinary infant soothing/sleep efficacy: Low
- Directness to narrow POE/NOWS treatment question: High

## 1. Research question

Whether low-level stochastic vibrotactile stimulation through a crib mattress reduces pharmacologic treatment and hospital burden in newborns with prenatal opioid exposure.

This is the strongest clinical efficacy layer currently in the MAJN evidence set, but its population and endpoints are narrow.

## 2. Design

Dual-site randomized clinical trial.

Enrollment:
- 208 mother/infant dyads
- 104 randomized to treatment as usual (TAU)
- 104 randomized to mattress SVS.

Analyzed after hospitalization completion at study site:
- 87 TAU
- 94 SVS
- total 181.

Infants were randomized within 48 hours of birth.

Intervention:
standard care plus low-level stochastic vibrotactile stimulation.

The mattress operated on a 3-hour ON/OFF cycle.

## 3. Population

Term newborns with prenatal opioid exposure.

Among the 181 analyzed:
- mean gestational age 39.0 ± 1.2 weeks
- mean birth weight 3076 ± 489 g.

All infants continued to receive standard clinical care.

Infants meeting clinical criteria for pharmacologic treatment were transferred to the NICU and received morphine according to standard care.

## 4. Primary outcomes

Pre-specified primary outcomes:
- administration of morphine treatment (AMT)
- cumulative morphine dose
- hospital length of stay.

The primary outcome is therefore not crying, sleep duration, settling time, or subjective comfort.

## 5. Main randomized results

Morphine treatment:
- TAU: 31/87 = 35.6%
- SVS: 29/94 = 30.9%
- P = .60

Therefore the randomized group assignment did NOT produce a statistically significant difference in treatment rate.

This point must be preserved in the evidence registry.

The study's stronger signal came from exposure duration and from the subset of infants who actually required morphine.

## 6. Exposure-duration association

After adjustment for site, sex, birth weight, opioid exposure and feed type, time spent on the SVS mattress was associated with lower likelihood of AMT.

Reported adjusted odds ratio:
0.88 per hour/day of SVS exposure
95% CI 0.81–0.93.

The authors translated average exposure of about 6 hours/day into an approximately 50% relative reduction in AMT.

Important:
this is an exposure-duration association inside the randomized study, not the same thing as the simple randomized-group comparison.

## 7. Treated responder subgroup

Among 32 infants who:
- received morphine
- completed treatment within 3 weeks,

SVS-assigned infants had:
- hazard ratio for completing treatment = 1.96
- 95% CI 1.01–3.81
- approximately 3.18 fewer treatment days
- approximately 1.76 mg/kg less cumulative morphine.

These are clinically interesting findings, but they are a subgroup analysis.

They should not be converted into a broad statement that all newborns benefit from vibration.

## 8. Important negative / null evidence

The trial provides a particularly important null result:

Randomized assignment did not significantly reduce the proportion of infants requiring pharmacologic treatment.

Therefore the correct evidence summary is not:

“Vibration significantly reduced morphine treatment.”

It is:

“Randomized assignment did not significantly change the overall treatment rate, while adjusted exposure-duration analyses and a treated subgroup showed favorable associations with treatment duration and morphine dose.”

This distinction should remain in the MAJN decision log.

## 9. Safety-related observation

The study had a safety protocol under which SVS was turned off 12–24 hours before anticipated discharge for observation.

Among 105 infants who received SVS, 58 had SVS turned off under this protocol; 6 (10.3%) met criteria for pharmacotherapy within 24 hours.

This observation is not evidence of a rebound adverse effect because the study was not designed to establish that causal interpretation.

It does demonstrate that device discontinuation and post-discontinuation observation were explicitly considered in the protocol.

## 10. Engineering exposure

The intervention is described as low-level stochastic vibrotactile stimulation through a specially constructed crib mattress.

The trial used the same general SVS platform lineage as the earlier 2009/2015/2017 work.

For the physical exposure definition, the earlier engineering studies remain more useful than this RCT because the 2023 paper's main contribution is clinical outcome rather than detailed surface characterization.

## 11. Evidence appraisal

### Evidence Type
Randomized clinical trial.

### Quality
High for the narrow clinical question, with important limitations in interpretation.

### Strengths
- randomized allocation
- two sites
- term newborn population
- clinically meaningful pharmacologic outcomes
- intention-to-treat analysis
- substantial sample relative to prior SVS studies.

### Limitations
- 27 infants did not complete hospitalization at study sites
- primary randomized treatment-rate comparison was not significant
- favorable duration/dose results are exposure-adjusted/subgroup findings
- population restricted to prenatal opioid exposure
- clinical outcome is not soothing/sleep
- device exposure is not isolated as a generic calming mechanism.

## 12. MAJN interpretation

This RCT changes the evidence level for a very specific question:

“Can SVS be clinically useful as a complementary intervention in term newborns with prenatal opioid exposure?”

It does not answer:

“Does vibration improve sleep or settling in healthy infants?”

Therefore RQ-VIB-03 must remain open.

## 13. MAJN research implications

### REQ-CLIN-11
Separate randomized assignment effects from dose/exposure-duration associations.

### REQ-CLIN-12
Predefine primary outcomes before data collection.

### REQ-CLIN-13
Do not promote subgroup outcomes to primary efficacy claims.

### REQ-CLIN-14
If discontinuation/rebound is scientifically relevant, define a prospective post-discontinuation observation window.

### REQ-CLIN-15
Keep clinical indication/population explicitly attached to every efficacy statement.

## 14. Evidence-chain position

2009:
engineering exposure + physiological proof-of-concept

2015:
randomized crossover physiological response

2017:
within-subject movement + cardiorespiratory response with synchronized event logging

2023:
larger randomized clinical outcome in POE newborns

FDA 2025:
regulatory risk/verification framework for a specific therapeutic device and indication.

This is a coherent evidence chain, but it is not a chain proving general infant soothing efficacy.

## 15. Decision

E-001-E is retained as high-value clinical evidence for the narrow POE/NOWS question.

The null overall treatment-rate result must be explicitly preserved.

For MAJN, the study supports:
- careful clinical endpoint definition,
- randomized design,
- exposure logging,
- subgroup/secondary-analysis discipline.

It does not support a general sleep/soothing claim.
