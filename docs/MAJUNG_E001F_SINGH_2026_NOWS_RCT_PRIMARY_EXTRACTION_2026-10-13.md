## 2. Design and population
Multicenter, prospective, open-label randomized trial. Neonates born at ≥35 weeks gestation, with antenatal opioid exposure and no NOWS signs at enrollment. Randomized 2:1 to SVS mattress or standard crib mattress. Of 129 assessed, 80 met eligibility and parents were approached; 62 enrolled (SVS n=40, control n=22). Planned enrollment was 120; study ended early on 2023-12-31 because of low enrollment. Both groups received standard non-pharmacologic care.

## 3. Intervention
Continuous low-level stochastic vibrotactile stimulation, 30–60 Hz, 10–12 μm RMS. Used until day 5 or initiation of pharmacotherapy. These are study-specific exposure parameters, not MAJUNG settings or universal safety limits.

## 4. Outcomes and results
**Primary endpoint — initiation of NOWS pharmacotherapy within 5 days**
- SVS: 4/40 (10.0%); standard: 4/22 (18.2%).
- Adjusted OR 0.54 (95% CI 0.10–2.85), P=.46.
- Adjusted risk difference −5.7% (95% CI −21.2% to 9.8%).
- The randomized comparison did not show a statistically significant difference; the confidence interval is wide and includes no benefit.

**Readiness for discharge**
- Median 3.9 days (SVS) vs 4.9 days (standard).
- Adjusted rate ratio 0.75 (95% CI 0.53–1.05), P=.09.
- Direction favored SVS but did not reach statistical significance.

**Cry and NOWS scores**
- FNASS/ESC scores did not differ between groups during the 5-day observation.
- ChatterBaby cry data were available for 46 infants (SVS n=32, standard n=14).
- Time in fussy/pain cry: median 8.0% vs 7.2%; Hodges–Lehmann estimator 0.3 (95% CI −1.5 to 2.3), P=.82.
- Cry strength: 87.2% vs 86.5%; estimator −0.8 (95% CI −3.9 to 2.8), P=.55.
- Some recording-duration subgroup comparisons were small and exploratory; do not treat isolated subgroup P values as confirmatory.

**Acceptability and adverse events**
- 60% of nurses and 80% of parents in the SVS group said the mattress helped soothe/calm the neonate. This is a subjective acceptability survey, not objective proof of reduced crying.
- Authors reported no adverse events in either study group. Bound this statement to the 62 infants and the short hospital observation context.

## 6. Evidence classification
- Evidence type: randomized clinical study of NOWS pharmacotherapy, discharge readiness, clinical scores, crying, and acceptability.
- Quality strengths: multicenter randomized allocation and central randomization.
- Limitations: open-label; planned n=120 vs actual n=62; early termination; 40:22 allocation; cry data only available for a subset; subjective acceptability.
- Directness to MAJUNG general healthy-infant soothing/sleep: low. Directness to SVS/NOWS research design: high.

## 7. MAJUNG interpretation boundary
This trial should be added to the evidence set. Its primary outcome, discharge-readiness comparison, cry outcomes and NOWS scores were not statistically significant. Caregiver acceptability is not a substitute for objective efficacy. The trial does not establish MAJUNG efficacy for healthy infants or long-term/home-use safety.
